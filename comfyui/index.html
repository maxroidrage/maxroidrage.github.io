ComfyUI & the Luna × Moonrise Sparkling case study

About this work

I’m an AI-native visual designer. I use ComfyUI every day, and I finish my work in Figma.

Designing with AI, step by step

I’m Bryan Sadler, a web designer and developer in Clarksville, Tennessee. For about three years I’ve built websites, applications, and APIs with AI and LLM tools, and generative AI is a regular part of my design work.

ComfyUI is where most of my image work starts. This page walks through one self-initiated project, Luna × Moonrise Sparkling, from the first character image to a finished video ad. It’s a mock brand I made to practice a full AI workflow. It is not a client project.

Case study

Luna × Moonrise Sparkling

A mock sparkling drink brand with its own mascot, logo, can, static ad, and video ad. I made the character, the brand, the packaging, and both ads myself with the tools listed at the bottom of this page. Here’s the process, one step at a time.

Step 01

Design the character in ComfyUI

Luna started as a single ComfyUI image: a wolf girl with teal hair, a white visor, a cropped tee, and olive cargo pants. I settled on her look first, because everything else in the project depends on it.

The first ComfyUI image of Luna.

Step 02

Build character sheets and a dataset with ChatGPT

I used ChatGPT to turn that first image into character sheets: expressions, music poses, and skateboard poses. Those sheets gave me a set of Luna images in many poses and expressions to use as training data.

Step 03

Train a character LoRA on the Anima model

I captioned each training image and trained a character LoRA on the Anima model, so Luna stays on-model in new poses and scenes. In ComfyUI I load Anima with the Luna LoRA and call her up with a trigger word.

A few of the captioned training images.

Step 04

Build the brand system

Next I built the Moonrise Sparkling brand around her: a badge logo, a wordmark, a color palette, and can packaging. Headlines use Russo One. Subheads and body text use Montserrat.

Step 05

Fix the can with Nano Banana

The LoRA gave me Luna holding a can, but the can was made up and its label was unreadable. I gave Nano Banana two inputs: the LoRA image and a render of the real Moonrise Midnight Berry can. I told it to swap in the exact can and keep the label, logo, and colors the same.

When I checked the result, I caught a finger-count error in her grip. I rewrote the prompt to spell out how the hand holds the can.

“Her paw grips the can naturally: four fingers wrap around the front-left side of the can and the thumb curls around the back.”
From my revised Nano Banana prompt

Before: LoRA output

After: Nano Banana fix

Step 06

Make the static ad in Figma

I built the static ad in Figma at 1200 × 628. It has the Moonrise wordmark, a headline, a short subhead, and a Shop Now button over Luna’s artwork.

The static ad, 1200 × 628.

Step 07

Animate in Kling, with Figma type on top

I started Kling from a square start frame and prompted art-only motion for 10 seconds: a head tilt, a wink, her hair moving in the breeze, then Luna dissolving into sparkles while the can stays in place.

The Kling clip has no text in it. I exported the Figma text layer as a transparent PNG and composited it over the clip into a 1200 × 628 MP4, so the brand text stays crisp.

The Kling start frame.
